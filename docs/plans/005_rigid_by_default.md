# Plan 005: Rigid by default

Evidence: [ER 005](/docs/engineering-records/005_rigid_by_default.md). Design: the [rigid-by-default brief](/docs/research/rigid-by-default-design-brief.md) (the formulas, thresholds, traps and tests this plan builds), the [core layout rules](/docs/research/core-data-layout.md) (binding: rules 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9 above all), the [spec](/docs/research/node-beam-engine-design-spec.md), and what milestone 4 left ([ER 004](/docs/engineering-records/004_parts_and_assembly.md), phase 4.11).

**What this milestone adds.** A node can ride on a rigid body. The engine and pod, already flagged never-live, become rigid bodies. Hinges, joint limits, servos and rotors join bodies or live lattices, and a simple wheel turns on a rotor. Segments that are barely loaded go to sleep (their nodes ride on a body) and wake under load, with linear and angular momentum unchanged across every switch. Rigid segments that share a seam merge into one body. A craft that breaks in two becomes two vessels. Sleeping is a cost saving, not a change to the game: the equivalence test (5.9) is the most important test in the milestone.

**Scope [assumption]:** still a test bed (Plan 002). The hopper from milestone 4, one tall rocket (a stack of tanks on the same generators), one test wheel, the craft page. No part editor beyond parameters, no staging, no fuel.

## Settled before building

These are decided and logged in [ER 005](/docs/engineering-records/005_rigid_by_default.md) (first dated section). A builder follows them; changing one is a logged decision with a reason, not a silent edit.

- **The core learns segments; it still never learns what a part is.** Segments, seams and the assembly tree reach the core as commands, built by the assembler that already has them. New per-node array `segment`; new per-segment arrays (flags, parent segment, seam to parent, stored mass); new per-seam arrays (the two segments, axial area, bending section). A beam's seam is derived from its endpoints' segments, not stored separately. Flat arrays, one per field (layout rule 3).
- **What was missing for sleeping (ER 004, 4.11) is settled here.** (1) **Segment mass is stored** when the segment's nodes are assigned (masses are constant: no fuel). **Centre of mass and inertia are not stored**; they are computed from the nodes' current positions at the moment of a switch, as the brief says, so a bent segment stays bent. (2) **Seam beams never become joints.** A seam beam is a beam: with both ends on the same body it is skipped; with one end riding and one live it is solved as normal through the riding endpoint; with both ends live it is solved as now. Joints are only what a part declares (hinge, limit, servo, rotor). No rule is needed beyond the endpoints' state at the start of the step.
- **One kernel.** The riding endpoint is a fourth case of the existing `Endpoint` in `core/src/solver.rs` (`Node`, `Fixed`, `Point`, and now `Riding(body, local offset)`). Its weight is `1/M + (r × n) · I⁻¹ · (r × n)` and a push moves and turns the body. Beams, ground contacts, beam-midpoint contacts, damping and joints all go through it; no second copy of the distance maths (layout rule 6).
- **Bodies are integrated in the island's substep loop** with nodes: predict, solve, derive velocity. Rotation is a unit quaternion, re-normalised each substep with the core's own square root. Inverse inertia is a full symmetric 3×3 in the body frame. The spin term (`ω × L`) is the explicit form with an angular-speed clamp **[assumption]**; the energy tests decide whether it stays.
- **A body lives in one island.** Island building treats every riding node of one body as joined, so a body never spans islands; a body with no live neighbours is an island of its own. The substep rule sees a beam to a riding node through the same weight, so a beam from a light live node to a heavy body is rated by the live node, which is what the solver sees.
- **Bodies are recomputed by a union-find over segments** on any step where a segment switched or a seam broke: the connected groups of rigid segments that share a live seam are the bodies, each named by its lowest segment index (brief). Merging two touching rigid segments is therefore not a separate feature: it is the same sum.
- **The switch formulas are the brief's, exactly.** Sleep: sum mass, centre, momentum, spin and inertia over the nodes and any body joining; positions unchanged; kinetic energy can only fall. Wake: each released node takes `V + ω × r`; the remaining body recomputes mass, centre and inertia and keeps `ω`; momentum, spin and kinetic energy unchanged. These are tested to rounding in the phase that builds them, and again on every switch in 5.8.
- **Thresholds start from the brief** (wake at an estimate above 25% of yield, sleep below 8% and nearly unchanging for 60 steps, minimum live time 30 steps, extra damping fading over 10 steps after a wake) **[assumption]**, tuned in 5.9 against the equivalence test only.
- **Contacts wake on arrival, not on resting [assumption].** A new contact on a segment, or a contact force on it above the wake level, wakes it and its neighbours before the solve (brief trap 2). A steady resting contact does not keep it awake, so a craft standing on the pad can sleep whole. Without this the legs, always touching the pad, would stay live and set the substep count on the ground (see the next item).
- **The substep count, honestly.** The hopper needs 19 substeps, and a leg alone also needs 19 (ER 004, 4.3). Making only the engine and pod rigid (5.4) therefore does not lower the count while the legs are live; the saving comes when legs and tank sleep: in flight, and standing at rest if the contact rule above holds. A hard landing wakes the legs and pays 19 substeps for those steps, which is correct: that is when the leg is doing work. **The legs are not retuned in this milestone**: 4.8's bands and the golden hop rest on them, and a leg's count is set by its own members and node masses, which sleeping does not change. 5.4 and 5.9 measure substeps per phase of a flight (standing, thrust, coast, touchdown); if the touchdown steps cost too much later, a stiffer-but-heavier or softer leg is a milestone 6 or later decision. ER 004's oracle candidate on this is answered here: the legs set the floor while live.
- **The mass ratio, measured where it now matters.** A rigid engine or pod is a body of hundreds of kilograms against live leg and tank nodes of 6.6 to 120 kg. What the solver sees is the body's weight at the point, not its total mass, so the ratio that matters is the live node's mass against the body's effective mass at each seam node (`1 / weight`). 5.3 measures how a light chain on a heavy body converges against the same chain on a pin; 5.4 reports the effective ratio at every seam of the hopper. The craft node-mass budget (20) stays a rule on live nodes; an effective-mass rule at seams is added only if 5.3 or 5.4 shows trouble.
- **Sleeping is a world setting, off by default** until 5.9 passes. `CMD_SET_SLEEPING` turns it on. (5.9 passed: the flight scenarios send it by default; the core's own default stays off.) Never-live segments become rigid through `CMD_MAKE_RIGID` regardless of the setting.
- **Goldens stay valid.** Recordings replay their own command bytes, and the committed ones (`weak-bridge.rcrd` ABI 2, `tower-collapse.rcrd` ABI 3, `hard-landing.rcrd` ABI 4, hop hash `0xd92f2aad01c0e048` at step 1320) send no new command, so they must replay unchanged at every phase. New state enters the state hash only when it exists: segment tables only once a segment command has run, bodies only while a body exists, joints only once one is added. Every phase's test list includes "the three goldens replay to their hashes". The live hop (compiled fresh, not replayed) keeps `0xd92f2aad01c0e048` until 5.4 makes the engine and pod rigid in flight scenarios; from then a second golden `hard-landing-rigid.rcrd` is recorded deliberately and logged, and the all-live golden stays as the regression for all-live mode (the flight scenarios keep a `rigid: false` option to rebuild it).
- **Commands and ABI.** Each phase that adds command kinds bumps `ABI_VERSION` (5 in 5.1, onward), lists the new kinds with their record layout in its record section, mirrors them in `src/commands.ts`, and keeps the recording decoder accepting every earlier ABI. New events (wake, sleep, split) follow rule 8: fixed records, island order.
- **Never-live parts carry their own flag through.** The engine and pod segments are rigid from spawn when the craft is compiled with `rigid: true`; they never wake. A wheel (5.6) is the same.

## Not in this milestone

- Retuning the leg to lower its substep count (see "Settled"; a later decision if touchdown steps cost too much).
- Leg deployment as a gameplay feature (a servo can drive one in a test; no deployable leg part).
- Lift and drag (milestone 6). Craft-against-craft and part-against-part contact (milestone 8): a broken-off vessel can still pass through its parent.
- Threads (milestone 8): bodies are designed per island so they slot in, but nothing runs in parallel here.
- Time warp and on-rails orbits; a floating origin.
- Wrecks in saves: offsets and bent rest lengths of sleeping segments are not written to the craft file.
- Real stiffness for sleeping segments (the open question in Plan 004 stays open).
- Fuel, staging, decouplers, steering and gimbals.

## Open questions

- [x] Does a steady resting contact really let the legs sleep without missing the start of a hard landing (5.8 and 5.9 decide)? 5.8: yes. A resting contact stays in range, so it is no arrival and the legs sleep on the pad; a foot coming into range, or closing faster than 0.25 m/s, wakes them before the solve. In the hop the legs asleep in flight wake at step 266 and 267, and the first yield is at step 267 with sleeping on and off (ER 005, phase 5.8). Whether the rest of the landing then matches is 5.9's equivalence test.
- [x] Is an effective-mass rule at seams needed on top of the craft node-mass budget (5.3 and 5.4 decide)? 5.3: not at 800 kg : 6.6 kg; a chain on a free heavy body settles like one on a pin (2.5e-11 relative) at the same substep count (ER 005, phase 5.3). 5.4: no rule needed. At the hopper's seams the body's effective mass `1 / w` along each seam beam is 0.88 to 4.02 times the live node's mass (engine) and 1.84 times (pod), far below the 121 of 5.3, and 4.8's bands pass rigid (ER 005, phase 5.4). The node-mass budget on live nodes stays the only rule.
- [x] Does the explicit spin term with a clamp hold energy on fast-tumbling debris, or is the implicit form needed (5.2 decides)? No: explicit with a clamp pumps energy up to the clamp, plain implicit bleeds spin; the implicit midpoint (Cayley) form keeps both to rounding and is the default (ER 005, phase 5.2).

### The ordering issue (rigid engine and pod)

- **[decision]** **Rigid bodies stay in milestone 5; the roadmap order is kept.** The brief recommended moving a minimal rigid body (riding nodes, no sleeping) to the start of milestone 4, because a heavy engine on a light truss is the mass-ratio problem. In this milestone the engine and pod are compact, fully braced lattices with their mass spread over their nodes, every node flagged never-live in the part data. Why: (1) the problem the brief names does not arise for the parts of this milestone: the hopper's engine nodes (about 44 kg) and tank nodes (about 50 kg) are nearly equal, and the generators enforce a node-mass ratio of at most 10 across a craft; (2) the riding endpoint changes the one correction kernel that beams and contacts share, and it is best designed once, with sleeping, merging and splitting, in milestone 5's Opus session, rather than twice; (3) the never-live flag, segments, seams and section properties are emitted now, so milestone 5 needs no change to any generator; (4) keeping the order needs no roadmap change. Cost: the engine and pod flex very slightly where a rigid body would not; 4.11 measures it. Plan 001's open question on this is answered "no" for now, but it is the owner's question, so it is left open there; **this is the one decision in this plan the owner may want to overrule.**

### Parts and assembly

- **[decision]** **Generators in TypeScript, emitting commands.** Same pattern as `src/design.ts` and `src/scenes3d.ts`: the core stays a solver of nodes and beams and never learns what a tank is. Parts and the assembler are pure functions, so Node tests cover them without the core.
- **[decision]** **No sine or cosine in generators.** Ring node counts are limited to 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 and 24, whose angles are multiples of 15° or 22.5°; their cosines and sines are built from `sqrt(2)`, `sqrt(3)` and the half-angle formula `cos(θ/2) = sqrt((1 + cos θ) / 2)`, all exactly rounded operations. Every JavaScript engine therefore gives the same bits, and the root yaw and the clock use the same table. **[assumption]** Six counts are enough for test-bed parts; others wait for a reason.
- **[decision]** **Part mass is a parameter; members set a floor.** Each member's own mass (density × area × length) goes half to each end node; the rest of the stated mass ("contents": propellant, equipment, crew) is spread evenly over the part's nodes. A stated mass below the members' own mass is refused. This keeps real densities meaningful without making a tank's mass depend on its wall alone.
- **[decision]** **Segments.** One segment per bay for tanks and trusses, one per leg, one each for engine and pod (never-live) **[assumption]**. A merged node keeps the parent's segment; the child's beams that touch it are seam beams. Each seam carries its section properties: axial area `Σ A` of the members crossing the cut, and bending stiffness `Σ A · d²` with `d` each member's distance from the cut's centroid. Emitted and tested in 4.2 and 4.4, used from milestone 5.
- **[decision]** **Welding merges nodes; ring beams come from the parent.** Two coincident nodes joined by a zero-length beam would be the stiffest beam in the craft and set the substep count; merging avoids that and keeps the node count down. Duplicate ring beams (both parts' perimeter and chords on the same merged nodes) would double the ring's stiffness, so the child's are dropped. Tolerance 1 mm on ring radius, refused above. The weld is done at build time, at rest (spec stability guard "weld only at rest").
- **[decision]** **The clock is a permutation.** Turning a child about the ring axis by `k` ring steps is a renumbering of its ring nodes plus the exact rotation from the table above; no angle is ever stored, so a saved craft rebuilds to the same bits.
- **[decision]** **Craft file in JSON with one canonical order** (format, version, name, parts in id order of insertion, joins in insertion order, root), keys in a fixed order, numbers by JavaScript's shortest round-trip conversion (specified exactly by ECMAScript, so the same on every engine). Strict reading: unknown fields are refused rather than ignored, so load-then-save can never silently drop something. Version 1. **[assumption]** The damage layer (spec: broken beams and bent rest lengths) waits for the open question on whether wrecks persist.

### Thrust

- **[decision]** **Thrusters are a core feature, not host forces.** The host could send `CMD_ADD_FORCE` every step, but it only reads 32-bit render positions, so the direction would differ from the core's own 64-bit one; a recording would carry one force command per thruster node per step; and milestone 5's wake triggers ("a thrust change on it") need the core to know what thrust is. With two command kinds a recording carries only throttle changes.
- **[decision]** **Thrust acts along tail-to-head, half on each node, constant through the step.** Direction from positions at the start of the step, applied through `add_force` (layout rule 7), so it costs one square root per thruster per step and nothing per substep. The engine generator puts the head and tail on the engine's axis and braces both to both rings, so the thrust spreads into the lattice. A thruster keeps pushing if its engine breaks off (that is physical); it stops only if a node is deleted or the two coincide.
- **[decision]** **Throttle is rejected outside 0 to 1, not clamped**, like every other bad record (deterministic rejection, nothing silently changed). No gimbal and no fuel flow in this milestone; the hopper's mass is constant.
- **[decision]** **ABI 4**, decoder accepts 2, 3 and 4: ABI 4 only adds command kinds, so older lists mean the same; the two committed goldens are the guard that nothing else moved.

### Numerics (decided here against performant, semi-realistic, deterministic)

- **[decision]** **Real materials, stiffness scaled by `s = 0.1` [assumption].** Real steel members at real sizes are far past the 32-substep cap (ER 002, phase 2.2). Scaling only the modulus, and deriving the strains from the real stresses, keeps the forces at which members yield and break real: `yield strain = σ_y / (s·E)`, so the yield force is `s·E·A · σ_y / (s·E) = σ_y · A`. The cost is that structures stretch ten times further under load and ring about three times slower (`sqrt(10)`), which reads as "a bit springy" and is the trade the spec's stiffness scaling already makes.

  | Material | E, GPa (scaled) | Density, kg/m³ | Yield, MPa | Yield strain | Break strain |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Steel | 200 (20) | 7850 | 350 | 0.0175 | 0.10 (ductile, about 6 × yield) |
  | Aluminium | 70 (7) | 2700 | 200 | 0.0286 | 0.08 (about 3 × yield) |
  | Carbon fibre | 120 (12) | 1600 | none (brittle) | 0.06 (never reached) | 0.05 (600 MPa strength) |

  **[assumption]** The break strains are game values, not real elongation at fracture (real ductile steel stretches 20% or more, which would look like toffee in a lattice).
- **[decision]** **Damping from a damping ratio.** Each beam gets `c = ζ · 2 · sqrt(k · m_a·m_b / (m_a + m_b))` with `k = EA / L`: `ζ = 0.05` for structure (the spec's minimum damping on every beam, enough to stop ringing without looking soft), `ζ = 0.6` for the sprung bay of a leg (no bounce).
- **[decision]** **Substep budget: at most 24 substeps for every default part and for the hopper** (cap 32, `c = 1`). The rule's `ω = sqrt(k · (w_a + w_b))` must stay under `24 × 60 = 1440`, so `EA ≤ 1440² · L · m / 2` for two equal nodes of mass `m`: about `3.2e7 N` for a tank member (L 0.625 m, 50 kg), `2.3e7 N` for an engine member (0.5 m, 44 kg), `6.5e6 N` for a leg member (0.5 m, 12.5 kg). At the scaled moduli those are areas up to about 46, 11 and 9 cm², and a real 4 mm aluminium tank wall of 1 m radius is about 31 cm² per longeron, so near-real parts fit.
- **[decision]** **Node-mass ratio at most 10 across a craft,** checked by the assembler (spec: mass limits in generators). The hopper's lightest nodes are leg nodes of about 12.5 kg, its heaviest merged ring nodes about 100 kg.

### The hopper (reference craft)

- **[decision]** Parts and numbers **[assumption]**, all tunable in 4.8 within the bands below:

  | Part | Mass | Shape | Material |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Pod | 1200 kg | bottom ring 8 nodes at r 1.0 m, top ring 8 at r 0.6 m, height 1.2 m, apex node (17 nodes) | aluminium, never-live |
  | Tank | 2000 kg (partly filled) | r 1.0 m, length 2.5 m, 5 rings of 8 (40 nodes, 4 bays) | aluminium |
  | Engine | 800 kg, 80 kN | top ring 8 at r 1.0 m, nozzle ring 8 at r 0.5 m, height 1.0 m, head and tail on the axis (18 nodes) | steel, never-live |
  | Legs, four | 150 kg each | on alternate sides of the tank's lowest bay; about 2.4 m long, foot 1.2 m out from the tank wall and 0.5 m below the nozzle, foot square 0.35 m, foot radius 0.15 m | aluminium |

  Total 4600 kg, weight 45.1 kN, thrust to weight 1.77, hover throttle 0.564. About 110 nodes and 550 beams after welding.
- **[decision]** **Leg in three stages,** from the foot up: a sprung bay, a crush bay, the upper leg. Per leg **[assumption]**: the sprung bay reaches the crush force after 0.25 m (about `1.8e5 N/m`, so the 11.3 kN static share compresses it about 6 cm), `ζ = 0.6`; the crush bay yields at **45 kN** (4 × the static share) over a **0.3 m** stroke and breaks at its end (its members get their own yield and break strains, a "crush cartridge", not aluminium's); the upper leg's members yield at no less than twice the crush force in axial load. Why a crush bay: a stiff leg meets the ground with a peak force of `v · sqrt(k · m)`, hundreds of kN even at 1 m/s, so without one either every landing yields the leg or nothing ever does; a constant-force crush gives a clear, tunable energy budget, uses only what the core already has (yield as a force cap, break on total strain in compression), and real landers use crushable cartridges. The sprung bay in series stops small landings from crushing at all, and once the crush force is reached it holds there, so it never over-compresses.
- **[decision]** **Why those numbers give the bands** (level touchdown, all four feet at once). The sprung bays store `4 × ½ × 45 kN × 0.25 m = 22.5 kJ` before anything crushes, but gravity does `45.1 kN × 0.25 m = 11.3 kJ` of work over that travel, so they absorb about 11.2 kJ of landing energy: the hopper's kinetic energy at about 2.2 m/s, hence no yield at 2 m/s. At 3 m/s (20.7 kJ) the remaining 9.5 kJ goes into the crush bays at a net `4 × (45 − 11.3) kN ≈ 135 kN`, about 7 cm of their 0.3 m stroke. The crush bays run out at about 51.6 kJ in total, a touchdown of about 4.7 m/s; at 7 m/s (113 kJ) at least one runs out of stroke and breaks, and on rough ground one leg lands first and takes most of it. A first sketch at 34 kN and 0.15 m left gravity's work out and would have crushed at 1.2 m/s; corrected here before any code. **[assumption]** The uneven split on rough ground and the damping's share are what 4.8 measures and tunes.
- **[decision]** **Acceptance bands for 4.8** (the test of fun): stands 20 s on the pad with no yield; touchdown at 2 m/s on the pad, no yield; at 3 m/s on the pad and on five fixed rough spots, no break and the craft stands (tilt under 15°); at 7 m/s on the same five rough spots, in at least four of them a leg snaps (its foot no longer joined to the pod through live beams) and the craft tips over (engine-tail-to-pod-apex axis more than 60° from vertical, at rest). Four of five, not one: the fun must not depend on one lucky spot. What a 7 m/s touchdown on the flat pad does is recorded, not required.
- **[decision]** **Lift-off check:** pod height gain after 3 s at full throttle within 5% of `½ (T/M − g) t² = ½ × 7.58 × 9 ≈ 34 m`, tilt under 5°, no yield. Quantitative on purpose: it proves the thrust is the stated thrust, not just "it goes up".
- **[decision]** **Drops for tests, a hop for the story.** Tests place the craft at rest above the ground so that it touches down at a chosen speed (free fall carries no internal load, so the start is clean and exact). The hop (throttle up, drift, cut, fall) is one scripted scenario for the demo and the golden. The hopper has no steering, so the drift comes from a small sideways "nudge" thruster across the tank at about the centre of mass height (3 kN **[assumption]**), which costs two commands in a recording instead of a force per node per step.
- **[decision]** **Landing field: 65 × 65 points at 0.5 m** (32 m square): a flat pad and beside it bumps 0.3 to 0.8 m high and 1 to 2 m across (2 to 4 cells per bump, so the triangles show their shape), made with milestone 3's `bump` and `smoothstep`. Size from the golden budget: 4225 heights are about 705 records, 45 KB; the hopper about 42 KB more; a hop recording stays under 150 KB. Friction 0.8 as before.
- **[assumption]** **No floating origin.** A hop goes up a few tens of metres and drifts a few metres; milestone 3's reasoning (32-bit render positions finer than 0.1 mm within a few hundred metres) holds.
- **[assumption]** **Contact cost.** ER 003 measured about 0.1 ms per step with contact solves up to half of beam solves for a structure lying on the ground. The hopper has about four times the beams of the tower; a tipped-over hopper is the worst case. 4.7 and 4.11 re-measure with `scene_stats`; no optimisation is planned.

### Numerics (decided here against performant, semi-realistic, deterministic)

- **[decision]** **Terrain is a height grid split into fixed triangles.** Two triangles per cell along the `(i, j)`–`(i+1, j+1)` diagonal. Why: each triangle has an exact plane, so height and normal are a few multiplies and one square root, the surface is continuous, and the renderer can draw the very triangles the physics uses (what you see touching is what touches). Bilinear patches were the alternative: smoother normals, but curved, so no exact plane to hold for a step, and the drawn triangles would not match. Outside the grid the edge heights continue flat, so a node that leaves the map still has ground instead of a cliff into nothing.
- **[decision]** `floor` is allowed in the terrain lookup. It is exactly specified by IEEE 754 (round to integral), gives the same bits natively and in WebAssembly, and is not on the determinism lint's list. A cast to integer would also do; `floor` reads more plainly for negative coordinates.
- **[decision]** **Terrain arrives as commands, not as a separate upload.** One `CMD_SET_TERRAIN` record (grid size in the two index fields; origin, cell size and friction in the numbers) and `CMD_TERRAIN_HEIGHTS` records of six heights each. Why: a recording stays "the command lists, nothing else" with an unchanged file format, and changing the terrain mid-run is replayable for free. Cost: a 257 × 257 grid is 11,009 records, about 700 KB in list 0 of a recording. The cap of 257 × 257 **[assumption]** keeps that bounded; milestone 7's larger worlds can add a bulk path then.
- **[decision]** **Heights are hashed in full each time the state hash is taken** (grid size, origin, cell, friction, then every height in index order). The hash is taken at checkpoints (every 60 steps) and in tests, so 66,049 extra mixes at most is cheap, and there is no cached checksum to get out of step.
- **[decision]** **ABI 3, ABI 2 recordings still accepted.** ABI 3 only adds command kinds, so every ABI 2 list means the same thing. Keeping the committed `weak-bridge.rcrd` untouched and requiring it to replay to `0xb580a6d8faa6610d` is the strongest available check that terrain and the new kernel change nothing when there is no ground.
- **[decision]** **Contact model as in the collision brief, applied to a static plane.** Found once per 60 Hz step per island; the plane of the triangle under the point is fixed for the step; the depth is re-measured against that plane every substep. Why fixed planes: the contact list can be reused across substeps without a terrain lookup in the inner loop, so the solver stays a pure function of the island buffer (threads in milestone 8), and the error from a node crossing into the next triangle within one step is second order in that step's travel. **[assumption]** That error is invisible at the scenes' speeds; 3.2's slope tests would show creep if not.
- **[decision]** **Margin** `|v|·Δt + ½·g·Δt² + 0.01 m` from the node's speed at the start of the step. The first two terms are the furthest it can travel toward the ground in one step from its own speed and gravity; the 1 cm skin covers what beams add within the step. The movement clamp bounds `|v|`. **[assumption]** The skin is a first guess; a contact that starts mid-step and is missed shows up as a node below the ground in 3.4's no-tunnelling check.
- **[decision]** **Rigid ground (no compliance).** The structure supplies the give, the ground does not. Soft ground with depth- and speed-dependent resistance is milestone 7's, and it enters through the same contact as a compliance, so nothing here blocks it.
- **[decision]** **Recovery cap 2 m/s** (the brief's figure). Per substep the target depth is `max(0, d₀ − 2 m/s · h)`, where `d₀` is the overlap at the substep's start: overlap that motion created this substep is removed in full, older overlap drains at no more than 2 m/s. This bounds the speed a deep spawn or a missed contact can be thrown out at, which is the main anti-kraken guard for contacts.
- **[decision]** **Friction is positional Coulomb with one coefficient** (static equals kinetic), after the push in the same pass: the sideways slip of the substep is cancelled in full if it is within `μ` times the push, otherwise cut by `μ` times the push. Why: on a node resting on the ground the push per substep is the weight's `g·h²`, so the limit is exactly `μ·N` and the slope test has a closed-form answer (stick below `tan θ = μ`, slide at `g (sin θ − μ cos θ)` above). Separate static and kinetic coefficients wait for surface types (milestone 7). Default `μ = 0.8` **[assumption]** (dry rock or concrete), set per terrain in the command.
- **[decision]** **No bounce.** Velocity is derived from position as for beams, so after the push the normal speed into the ground is gone; there is no restitution term to tune. Structures land, they do not bounce.
- **[decision]** **Contacts are solved after the beams in each substep,** so every substep ends with the ground satisfied and the beams carrying the reaction into the next one. Beams after contacts would let the last beam pass push nodes back under the ground at the end of every substep.
- **[decision]** **One correction kernel for beams and contacts** (layout rule 6). The kernel takes two endpoints (a node, a static point, or from 3.3 a weighted point on a beam), a direction and a constraint value, and moves the endpoints by inverse mass. `solve_distance` keeps its yield cap and calls the kernel; a contact calls it with a fixed normal and a push-only clamp. The beam path must give the same bits as now: the golden recording and the benchmark scene's hash `0x871092299f3cea56` are the guard.
- **[decision]** **Beam midpoints, from 3.3.** One sample per live beam at weights ½, ½, with the smaller end radius, listed after all node contacts in ascending beam order (contacts sorted by feature index, brief trap 4). One sample, not several: 3 m members over 1 to 2 m cells are covered by ends plus midpoint, and the cost is one extra contact check per beam per step. **[assumption]** More samples if 3.4 shows beams sinking into ridges.
- **[decision]** **Materials and radius for the scenes.** Milestone 2's strong member (`EA` 2e8 N, 100 kg per node, damping 5000 N·s/m, yield 0.00125, break 0.01), which stayed at 20 substeps, under the cap of 32. Node radius 0.15 m. Weak variants are the same member at a tenth, as in milestone 2. **[assumption]** A test bed's numbers, not real steel (ER 002, phase 2.2, explains why real steel members exceed the cap).
- **[decision]** **Structures are not pinned to the ground.** Feet and bridge ends rest on the terrain and are held by weight and friction. That is the roadmap's "with friction", and a knocked tower can tip and slide as a body, which is what makes a collapse look real.

### Rendering

- **[decision]** **Three.js pinned, vendored, import map, no bundler.** `three` at one exact version in `package.json`; the build copies the module files from `node_modules` into `dist/web/vendor/`; the page maps the bare imports with an import map. ER 002 said to revisit the no-bundler choice when Three.js arrived; an import map needs no new tool and nothing generated is committed, so it stays. No CDN: the page works offline and the version cannot move underneath a test.
- **[decision]** **`WebGPURenderer` with TSL,** using its own WebGL 2 fallback. The spec uses TSL for particles, debris and ball water (milestone 9); starting on `WebGLRenderer` would mean porting the skinning shader then. **[assumption]** The pinned version's TSL names are checked in its docs at phase 3.5; if TSL blocks 3.8, falling back to `WebGLRenderer` with a plain shader is a logged decision there.
- **[decision]** **Skinning by four-node barycentric binding, per cell.** Each panel vertex is a fixed weighted sum of four non-coplanar nodes of its cell. Why: it is linear in node positions, so it is exact for any rigid motion and any uniform stretch of the cell, needs no rotation extraction (no polar decomposition, no quaternions), and is four texture reads and a weighted sum in the vertex shader. Normals turn by the cofactor of the cell's deformation (cross products of the deformed edges). Per-cell panels part at cell edges when the lattice does, so a collapse looks torn along member lines without milestone 9's seams. **[assumption]** Panels whose cell is crushed flat will look crushed; that is honest, not a bug.
- **[decision]** **Render interpolation** between the last two render buffers, by the step clock's fraction. Render only: the core is still stepped by whole steps (ER 002, phase 2.3, deferred this to 3D).
- **[decision]** No floating origin yet: scenes sit within a few hundred metres of the origin, where 32-bit render positions resolve better than 0.1 mm. ER 002's **[assumption]** stands until milestone 4's flights need more.

### Known limits carried into the milestone

- **[question]** Debris passes through debris: there is no node or beam contact between structures or within one until milestone 8. "Collapse believably" is judged with that in mind; if it reads as wrong, the cure is milestone 8's, not a milestone 3 workaround.
- **[question]** A pin-jointed lattice does not buckle as a slender column does; a tower fails by members yielding in compression (the force cap) and by tipping. Expected to look like a structure crumpling at its joints. Judged in 3.6.
- **[decision]** No `[oracle-candidate]` is nominated: the contact model and its numbers come from the collision brief, and each uncertain number above has a test in the plan that will measure it.

## 2026-11-17 (phase 3.2, ground contact and friction)

- **[action]** Built by Opus. `solver.rs`: `Endpoint` (`Node`, `Fixed`; 3.3 adds the weighted point), the kernel `apply_correction`, `RECOVERY_SPEED`, and `solve_ground_contact` (push, split recovery, friction); `solve_distance` now moves its ends only through the kernel; the substep runs the contacts after the beams, before the velocity is derived. `core/src/contact.rs`: `find_contacts` (a pure function of the island buffer and the terrain), `CONTACT_SKIN`. `Island` gains `radius`, `contact_node`, `contact_normal`, `contact_offset` and `friction`; `StepStats` gains `contacts`. Nothing changes for a world without terrain beyond an empty loop.
- **[action]** Tests: `core/tests/ground.rs` (7, one ignored), `terrain_scene_through_the_boundary_matches_the_direct_world` in `core/tests/ffi.rs` and its twin in `test/wasm.test.ts` (golden `0x05e2e896a698c2f5`: a triangle dropped on the bumpy 5 × 4 terrain, a node spawned under a slope, a node spawned under the ground past the grid corner; 120 steps). `cargo test`, `cargo test --release -- --ignored` for the ten-minute rest, `pnpm test` (27 pass, 1 slow skipped), `RIBCAGE_SLOW=1` (the 720-step benchmark in WebAssembly and `0xb580a6d8faa6610d`) and `pnpm typecheck` pass.
- **[action]** Kernel bit check: the benchmark end hash is `871092299f3cea56` before and after the kernel, the golden recording replays unchanged, every earlier golden in `ffi.rs` and `wasm.test.ts` is unchanged. Step time on the benchmark scene 6.3 to 6.5 ms against 6.66 ms before (run-to-run noise; the kernel is inlined and does the same arithmetic in the same order).
- **[action]** Measured: a node dropped from 2 m lands at step 38 at exactly `radius` (to the bit), with no upward speed afterwards and no sideways slide. A node spawned 0.5 m deep rises at exactly 2 m/s and is out at step 15, with no hop. The 2 m lattice cube (8 × 100 kg, all 28 pairs braced, `EA` 2e8 N, 24 substeps) on `tan θ = 0.3`, `μ = 0.5` settles 8.7 µm downhill in the first second (elastic settling of the lattice) and then moves 1e-16 m in 9 s. On `tan θ = 0.9` it slides at 2.916686 m/s², the closed form `g (sin θ − μ cos θ)` to all seven printed digits. Resting on the gentle slope for ten minutes, its energy after settling never rises at all (max rise 0 J, tolerance 2.1e-9 J) and it does not creep (0 m).
- **[decision]** **Split recovery** (a refinement of the 2 m/s cap above). The part of a push that removes overlap already present at the substep's start (at most `d₀ − allowed`) also moves the substep's start position along the normal, so the velocity derived from position does not include it. Why: without it a node dug out of the ground leaves with up to 2 m/s upward and hops about 0.2 m; that would make every missed contact, terrain edit under a structure or deep spawn pop debris into the air. With it the node rises at the capped rate and stops at the surface. Only the part of the push that stops the node's own motion into the ground changes its velocity, so landing still stops a falling node dead. The same split is what a resting contact's ulp-level overlap goes through, so resting nodes do not pick up a tiny upward speed either. This is the split-impulse idea in positional form.
- **[decision]** **Friction normal includes the recovery push.** The friction limit is `μ` times the whole push, so a node digging out of the ground is held harder sideways than its weight alone would hold it. Why: it is simpler, it only acts while a node is in a state that should not exist, and it keeps a node from skating along under the surface while it recovers. Resting and sliding contacts have no recovery part, so the closed-form slope answers are untouched.
- **[decision]** **Contact is rigid** (no compliance on the push) and **push-only** (a contact never pulls a node down). Pushes and friction go through the shared kernel with the ground as the `Fixed` end, so 3.3's weighted beam point plugs in by adding an `Endpoint` variant, with no second copy of the maths.
- **[decision]** **The contact plane is taken under the node's position at the step's start** and held for the step, as planned. The gap uses the plane's signed distance, not the height difference, so on a slope the node rests at `radius` measured along the normal. `|g|` in the margin is the length of `gravity_at(p)`, so a later non-vertical gravity changes nothing here.
- **[decision]** **Contacts are solved in the solver's single pass,** after all the beams and before the velocity is derived, in ascending packed node order. No second contact pass: the resting cube shows no jitter or creep with one. The open question on resting piles stays open until 3.3's beam midpoints and 3.4's "comes to rest" tests, which are the real pile.
- **[question]** The speed clamp re-places a clamped node at `p0 + v·h` after the contact solve, which can put it back under the ground for one substep; the next substep's push handles it (as old overlap, at 2 m/s). Not seen in any test (no node clamped); noted for 3.4's collapse runs.
- **[question]** The roadmap check list is unchanged by 3.2 (the checks are proven in 3.4 to 3.8), so the percentages stay. The plan's demo for 3.2 (a cube sticking beside one sliding, from a trace) was not made; the two slope tests print the numbers it would show, and 3.5's page shows it live.

## 2026-11-17 (phase 3.3, beam midpoints on the ground)

- **[action]** Built by Sonnet. `solver.rs`: `Endpoint::Point(a, b, t)` (the point `(1 − t)·p_a + t·p_b` on a beam) with its weight `b_a²·w_a + b_b²·w_b`, `Endpoint::place`, and a private `shift` that moves each of its nodes by `b_i·w_i·Δλ`; `apply_correction` now only calls `shift` on its two ends. `solve_ground_contact` takes an `Endpoint` and the start-position array instead of one node and one start position, so a node contact and a midpoint contact are the same code. `contact.rs` finds beam contacts after the node contacts, in ascending beam order. `Island` gains `beam_contact`, `beam_contact_normal`, `beam_contact_offset`; `StepStats` gains `beam_contacts` (`contacts` still counts nodes only).
- **[action]** Tests: `core/tests/midpoint.rs` (5): a 3 m beam dropped across a one-cell plateau rests on it with its ends 0.5 m in the air, level, midpoint at exactly plateau plus radius; the same beam dropped flat with end masses 1 and 3 kg lands level (ends equal to the bit) with two node contacts and one beam contact; a beam with a pinned end leaves the pinned node alone and nothing sinks; the weighted endpoint moves its nodes by their shares (the point itself by weight times `Δλ`, both as first and as second end); the same run twice gives the same hash. Both beam runs also check the ground's impulse step by step: it is never downward (least value −1.3e-11 N·s, rounding) and over the run it sums to weight times time within 1e-6. `cargo test`, the ten-minute rest in release, `pnpm test` (27 pass, 1 slow skipped), `RIBCAGE_SLOW=1` (benchmark scene in WebAssembly, 6.57 ms mean per step, hash unchanged) and `pnpm typecheck` pass.
- **[action]** Bit check: with no terrain nothing changed (benchmark `871092299f3cea56`, the golden recording, the small bench and hand-built goldens, and the terrain-only world all as before). 3.2's ground tests pass unchanged, including the slope accelerations to the same seven digits and the resting cube's zero energy rise and zero creep over ten minutes: the bottom face's beam midpoints lie in the contact plane, so they add no push there.
- **[decision]** **Golden re-recorded:** `TERRAIN_SCENE_120` is now `0x2bdef035f12d0c86` (was `0x05e2e896a698c2f5`) in `core/tests/ffi.rs` and `test/wasm.test.ts`. Why: that scene's triangle rests with its three beams on the ground, so their midpoints now push, which is the new behaviour; it is the only golden that moved.
- **[decision]** **Midpoint rules as planned, with two details.** The contact test uses the midpoint's own position and speed (mean of its two nodes) and the smaller end radius; a beam whose two nodes are both pinned gets no contact (it cannot be pushed). A beam with one pinned end does, and the free node takes the push alone through its share (the midpoint resists with `¼·w`, so it moves half as far as the node).
- **[decision]** **Split recovery for a point** moves each node's start position by its share of the recovered push (`b_i·w_i·recovered / W`), the same as the push moved the nodes, so a beam dug out of the ground by its midpoint does not leave with the recovery speed. For a node the arithmetic is exactly what 3.2 had.
- **[decision]** **Friction acts on the midpoint's own slip** (the sideways part of its move over the substep), through the same kernel. A beam rotating about its midpoint is not slowed by it; the end contacts do that.
- **[question]** Open pile question (plan, line 92): still not measured. 3.3's resting cases are single beams; the real pile is 3.4's collapse and rest tests.
- **[question]** The roadmap check list is unchanged by 3.3 (the checks are proven in 3.4 to 3.8).

## 2026-11-17 (phase 3.4, tower and bridge on terrain, headless)

- **[action]** Built by Sonnet. `src/terrain.ts`: `Terrain`, `flatTerrain`, `compileTerrain`, `heightAt` (the core's triangle rule, flat past the edges), `bump`, `smoothstep` and two generators using only `+ - * /`, `sqrt` and `floor` (no `sin`, `cos` or `pow`, so every engine gives the same bits). `plainTerrain()`: 41 × 41 points, 1 m cells, five hills up to 0.8 m, dead flat within 4 m of the origin. `valleyTerrain()`: 61 × 31 points, 1 m cells, two ledges at height 0 (`x ≤ 2` and `x ≥ 22`), smoothstep sides 6 m wide down to a floor 5 m below, four floor bumps up to 0.7 m.
- **[action]** `src/scenes3d.ts`: the tower (six storeys of 3 m on a 4 m square, 28 nodes, 114 beams: four columns and crossed diagonals on every face per storey, a ring with two diagonals at every level) and the bridge (eight 3 m bays, 3 m deep, trusses 3 m apart: two Warren trusses, cross beams, crossed diagonals in the deck and roof planes, sway braces). Every beam is the strong member of `bridge.ts` (`EA` 2e8 N, 100 kg per node, radius 0.15 m), 20 substeps. Nothing is pinned: feet and bridge ends rest on the ground by weight and friction. Actions are command lists: `towerAction` and `bridgeAction` (`knock`, `removeColumn`, `overload`), all starting at step 120 so the scene first takes its own weight; the bridge's standard truck crosses in every run except the overload.
- **[action]** `test/scenes3d.test.ts` (11 tests): both scenes stand 20 s with no yield, break or freeze and end below 1 mm/s; each of the six scene and action pairs breaks beams, never puts a node more than radius + 1 cm below the ground, freezes nothing, stays far below the clamp speed, and is at rest (every node below 1 mm/s) within 35 s (tower) or 50 s (bridge) of the action; the tower knock and the bridge overload give the same hash run twice and a replay of the saved byte lists matches; the terrains are flat where the scenes sit and give the same grid twice. `cargo test`, `pnpm test` (38 pass, 1 slow skipped) and `pnpm typecheck` pass. No core change: every golden is as before.
- **[decision]** **Action numbers** (test-bed [assumption]s, tuned until each action collapses its scene and the debris comes to rest):
  - Tower knock: 3e4 N along +x split over the four roof corners for 0.5 s (about 26 yields, 3 breaks, at rest about 10 s later). A first, larger knock was far too violent (up to 180 m/s).
  - Tower removeColumn: the bottom storey's columns and the diagonals on them at two neighbouring corners (a set, since neighbours share diagonals), about 22 yields, 2 breaks, at rest about 16 s later.
  - Tower overload: 2e6 N downward over the roof corners, ramped in over 2 s, held 3 s, then removed (about 101 yields, 77 breaks, at rest about 10 s after). A load held for ever keeps pressing on the debris and never lets it settle (0.016 m/s creep), and a first, larger load threw debris at 650 m/s.
  - Bridge knock: 2e5 N along +z on the two middle deck nodes for 0.5 s (21 yields, 2 breaks), the slowest to settle, about 34 s after the action.
  - Bridge removeColumn ("cut"): the middle bay's two bottom chords and its two deck diagonals (the chords alone changed nothing, the deck diagonals carried the load), 10 yields, 6 breaks, at rest in about 12 s.
  - Bridge overload: `HEAVY_TRUCK`, 200 t at 4 m/s (a 30 t truck did not hurt it), 114 yields, 99 breaks, at rest in about 18 s.
- **[decision]** **Rest means 1 mm/s from position changes** over one step, for every node. The test cannot read node speed through the boundary, and a position change over a step is the speed the next step starts from.
- **[decision]** **Clamping is checked by the observed speed, not the count.** The clamped-node count and node velocity are not exposed through the boundary, and an ABI change was out of scope. The test asserts the fastest observed node stays under 500 m/s, half the 1000 m/s clamp. In the six collapses the fastest was up to about 235 m/s (the overload's falling debris), so no clamp is expected. If a later phase needs the exact count, it goes through the boundary.
- **[decision]** **"Below the ground" is measured at the node centre,** `y − heightAt(x, z)`, and the test requires it above `−(radius + 1 cm)`, the plan's literal figure. The one scene that goes below ground at all is the bridge overload: its centre reaches 7.7 cm below the ground height (step 451, one node of a 200 t pile, moving 41 m/s) before the contact pushes it out. That is a landing node against a slope in a pile-up, not a drift, and it is inside the plan's limit but not inside a strict "centre never below the surface". The tower's lowest gap stays positive (0.142 m above ground in its overload).
- **[question]** **Open pile question** (plan, line 92): the real piles now exist, and nothing creeps or jitters: every collapse reaches below 1 mm/s and stays there to the end of the run (the bridge overload shows about 1e-4 m/s of residual motion for a few seconds after the pile settles, then none). One contact pass per substep is enough for these scenes, so the second pass or the larger skin is not needed yet. The question stays open for the denser piles of milestones 4 to 8.
- **[question]** The speed-clamp-after-contact case noted in 3.2 was not seen: no clamp is expected in any run (see the observed-speed limit above).
- **[question]** The roadmap check "a tower and a bridge stand on height-map terrain with friction" is ticked here (milestone 3 at 25%, overall 24.5%); it is seen on the page in 3.5.

## 2026-11-17 (phase 3.8, meshes skinned to the nodes)

- **[action]** Built by Opus. `src/skin.ts`: a vertex is bound to one tetrahedron of its cell (four nodes not in one plane) by barycentric weights `wᵢ = (cᵢ · d) / det` with `c₁ = e₂×e₃`, `c₂ = e₃×e₁`, `c₃ = e₁×e₂`, `w₀ = 1 − w₁ − w₂ − w₃`; its normal is stored as `mᵢ = (eᵢ · n) / det` and skinned as `m₁ (e₂'×e₃') + m₂ (e₃'×e₁') + m₃ (e₁'×e₂')`, which is the cofactor of the cell's deformation applied to the rest normal. `skinMesh` is the float64 reference, `shadeVertexF32` repeats the shader op for op in 32-bit floats, `packNodeTexture` fills the node texture.
- **[action]** `src/panels3d.ts`: the tower has one cell per storey (8 tetrahedra, one anchored at each corner: the corner, its two ring neighbours, the corner above or below) carrying its floor slab and four wall bands, the top storey also the roof slab (31 panels, 744 vertices); the bridge has one cell per bay (4 tetrahedra, one per deck corner, each closed by the top node of its own truss, since the deck nodes alone are in one plane) carrying one deck slab (192 vertices). Panels are flat-shaded boxes.
- **[action]** `web/viewer3d.ts` / `web/terrain.html`: one panel mesh per scene with a `MeshStandardNodeMaterial`; the TSL `positionNode` reads the vertex's four nodes from the node texture (`textureLoad`), returns `Σ wᵢ pᵢ` and passes the cofactor normal to `normalNode` through a varying. The drawn (interpolated) node positions are uploaded once per frame. New View checkboxes "beams" and "panels" (both on), hooks `?beams=0` and `?panels=0`. The truck now stands on the deck slab.
- **[action]** `test/skin.test.ts` (12 tests): every vertex binds to four different nodes of its own cell with weights summing to 1, and each panel to one cell; the rest pose gives the mesh within 1e-12 (positions and normals); three rotations with moves give the rotated mesh within 1e-6 m (normals 1e-9); three stretches (uniform, unequal, shear) give the stretched mesh within 1e-6 m and the cofactor normals within 1e-9; the 32-bit shader copy agrees with the reference within 1e-4 on rest, rotated and non-affine (every node pushed its own way) lattices; the texture layout; in the knocked tower's 600-step collapse no vertex moves more than 5 cm further from its anchor node than at rest (worst measured 3.0 cm). `pnpm test` (72 pass, 1 slow skipped), `pnpm typecheck` and `cargo test` pass; the core is untouched, every golden is as before.
- **[action]** Checked in the browser pane, WebGPU and WebGL 2 (`?webgl=1`): the floors and walls tilt with the knocked tower and lie on the ground with it at step 500; the bridge deck sags into the valley under the 200 t truck with beams hidden; no console errors. The pane was hidden, so the runs were stepped with the Step key rather than played.
- **[decision]** **One tetrahedron per vertex, not a blend.** The tetrahedron whose anchor node is nearest the vertex at rest (first one on a tie). Exact for any affine motion of the cell, cheap (four texture reads), and a panel near a corner follows that corner. Panels part at cell borders when cells part; that reads as a structure coming apart until milestone 9 tears seams properly.
- **[decision]** **Node texture: RGBA float32, 256 wide, node i at (i mod 256, i / 256), read with `textureLoad` (no filtering).** One upload per frame of the drawn positions; the beams and panels show the same interpolated nodes.
- **[decision]** **Tolerances:** 1e-12 at rest (float64 rounding), 1e-6 m under rotation and stretch (the plan's), 1e-4 between the 32-bit shader and the reference (32-bit floats near 60 m are 4e-6 m apart; a handful of roundings stays far below a millimetre).
- **[decision]** **Panel sizes are drawing assumptions:** floor slabs inset 0.12 m and 0.16 m thick, wall bands 0.95 m tall standing 0.2 m out from the nodes, deck slabs 0.2 m thick with a 4 cm gap between bays. They only have to clear the drawn beams and read as floors, walls and a road.

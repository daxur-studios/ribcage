## 2026-11-16 (phase 1.3)

- **[decision]** Damping in the distance solve is `γ = compliance · damping / h` (the XPBD paper's form, damping in newton-seconds per metre), not the layout rules' first `γ = (compliance / h²) · damping / h`, which made damping grow with the square of the substep count. Owner approved; `core-data-layout.md` corrected.
- **[action]** Phase 1.3 done: `core/src/solver.rs` has the one `solve_distance`, `solve_island` (predict, one pass over the beams in order, velocity from movement; a pure function of the packed island) and `substep_count` (smallest n with ω_max·h ≤ c, min 4, max 32). `step` uses the rule and returns the count; `step_with_substeps` forces a count for demos and comparisons. `last_lambda` holds the last substep's tension in newtons. **[assumption]** c = 1, not measured; phase 1.4 measures it. Tests: a hanging mass settles at weight times compliance (exact), ten times stiffer stretches ten times less, a pendulum keeps its length and gains no height, a ten-link chain settles and converges with substeps, the substep count follows the rule, same inputs give the same bits and hash. `cargo test` passes (12 tests). No roadmap check proven. [demo] `cargo run -p beam-core --example trace -- brace` (open square folds about a second after a 3 N push, braced one holds) and `-- substeps` (4 m loaded truss at 1 and 4 substeps collapses, at 16 and the rule's 24 holds with about 0.1 m of sag and light ringing); player now shows substeps per pane and skips hash comparison for scenes that differ on purpose. Checked in the browser; no clip recorded yet.
- **[finding]** For 1.4: with one pass per substep, a chain or truss settles softer than the textbook and the excess shrinks roughly in proportion to 1/substeps (ten-link chain top beam: 2.2x at 8, 1.7x at 11, 1.15x at 32). Damping adds to that settled sag (1.05x at 32 with none), because the damping term sees each sweep's half-solved positions. A single beam is exact at any damping. So the cantilever's damping value moves its measured deflection; settle it with low damping, or decide whether damping should move to a separate velocity pass.

## 2026-11-16 (phase 1.4)

- **[action]** Phase 1.4 done: `core/tests/support/truss.rs` generates the long square truss (four rails, square frames, a diagonal on every face and across every frame; standard one 6 m long, 0.5 m square, EA 360 kN, 1 kg nodes, 200 N on the tip), gives the textbook tip (bending `Q a² (3L − a) / (6 EI)` with `EI = EA · w²`, plus the side faces' shear by the unit-load method, superposed over the tip load and each frame's weight; derivation in the doc comment) and an exact static solve of any world (Newton's method on the force balance). `core/tests/cantilever.rs` has three tests and one ignored measurement. `cargo test` passes (15 tests, 1 ignored). Roadmap: cantilever check ticked, milestone 1 at 43%, overall 7%. [demo] `cargo run -p beam-core --example trace -- cantilever`, player at `?t=cantilever-08,cantilever-16,cantilever-rule,cantilever-32`: the truss settles at 8, 16, the rule's 20 and 32 substeps against an orange textbook-tip mark (tip centre y −0.082 m; settles at −0.912, −0.294, −0.216, −0.131). Player draws optional trace `marks` and prints the marked nodes' height. Checked in the browser; no clip recorded yet.
- **[finding]** Formula against the exact truss: textbook 0.3318 m, exact linear 0.3283 m (+1.1%), exact nonlinear 0.3268 m (+1.5%). The truss is statically indeterminate; that is the formula's own error.
- **[finding]** Solver error is `K · (ω_max · h)²`, not `1/substeps` as the 1.3 finding guessed. Standard truss, settled tip over exact, beam damping zero: 8.9 at 4 substeps, 3.56 at 8, 1.67 at 16, 1.43 at the rule's 20, 1.17 at 32, 1.07 at 48, 1.04 at 64, 1.01 at 128, 1.003 at 256. Excess times `n²` is constant (about 171). `K` is 0.42 on this truss, 0.41 on the 1.3 chain, 0.42 on the 1.3 flat truss, 0.37 and 0.39 on square trusses 6 and 24 times as long as wide. The same truss scaled 16 times larger (ω four times lower) gives identical ratios at the same `ω h`. Solve order barely matters (members reversed: about 1%).
- **[finding]** Stability does not limit `c`: the test truss stays bounded at every count down to one substep (`ω h` = 20, where it sags 18 times too far); the chain at 4 and the flat truss at 8 (`ω h` about 2.6 and 2.9) too.
- **[finding]** Beam damping adds sag on top, as 1.3 found: ratio at 20 / 32 substeps is 1.427 / 1.167 with none, 1.434 / 1.172 at 10 N·s/m, 1.498 / 1.212 at 100, 2.13 / 1.61 at 1000. Beam damping alone also leaves the first bending mode (about 10 rad/s) ringing for over 40 s, because damping on stiff beams barely touches slow modes.
- **[decision]** Settling method: beam damping zero and a test-side drag `−10 · v` newtons per node through `add_force`. It is zero at rest, so it cannot move the settled shape (results identical at drag 5, 10 and 20). It lags one 60 Hz step and pumps the fastest modes above about 100 substeps, so the 128-substep test switches it off after 3 s. The solver's damping is unchanged; whether damping moves to a separate velocity pass stays open, not needed for this test.
- **[decision]** Tolerances, from the measurements: formula within 2% of exact (measured 1.5%); solver at 128 substeps within 2% of the formula (measured −0.5%: the formula's +1.5% and the solver's +1.0% partly cancel); at the rule's count, the law itself: excess over exact between `0.38 (ω h)²` and `0.46 (ω h)²` at 16, 20, 32 and 64 substeps (measured 0.415 to 0.43), and shrinking. Not the 10% guess: at the rule's count the error is about 43%, and a percentage would hide that it is a known law of the method.
- **[decision]** `c` stays 1.0. It is not a stability limit; it is the cost and accuracy dial, with excess about `0.4 c²` at the rule's count. Halving it (10%) doubles the solver cost and pushes the test truss to 40 substeps, past the maximum of 32. Raising the maximum and the game-wide stiffness scale are owner calls, so this is surfaced rather than changed.
- **[question]** Accept about 40% softer-than-nominal structures at `c = 1` (tune stiffness by feel through the stiffness scale), or buy accuracy with `c = 0.5` and a maximum of 64 substeps at twice the cost? Recommended: keep `c = 1` until the 1.7 benchmark gives the cost, then decide.

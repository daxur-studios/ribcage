Roadmap: cantilever check ticked, milestone 1 at 43%, overall 7%. [demo] `cargo run -p beam-core --example trace -- cantilever`, player at `?t=cantilever-08,cantilever-16,cantilever-rule,cantilever-32`: the truss settles at 8, 16, the rule's 20 and 32 substeps against an orange textbook-tip mark (tip centre y −0.082 m; settles at −0.912, −0.294, −0.216, −0.131). Player draws optional trace `marks` and prints the marked nodes' height. Checked in the browser; no clip recorded yet.
- **[finding]** Formula against the exact truss: textbook 0.3318 m, exact linear 0.3283 m (+1.1%), exact nonlinear 0.3268 m (+1.5%). The truss is statically indeterminate; that is the formula's own error.
- **[finding]** Solver error is `K · (ω_max · h)²`, not `1/substeps` as the 1.3 finding guessed. Standard truss, settled tip over exact, beam damping zero: 8.9 at 4 substeps, 3.56 at 8, 1.67 at 16, 1.43 at the rule's 20, 1.17 at 32, 1.07 at 48, 1.04 at 64, 1.01 at 128, 1.003 at 256. Excess times `n²` is constant (about 171). `K` is 0.42 on this truss, 0.41 on the 1.3 chain, 0.42 on the 1.3 flat truss, 0.37 and 0.39 on square trusses 6 and 24 times as long as wide. The same truss scaled 16 times larger (ω four times lower) gives identical ratios at the same `ω h`. Solve order barely matters (members reversed: about 1%).
- **[finding]** Stability does not limit `c`: the test truss stays bounded at every count down to one substep (`ω h` = 20, where it sags 18 times too far); the chain at 4 and the flat truss at 8 (`ω h` about 2.6 and 2.9) too.
- **[finding]** Beam damping adds sag on top, as 1.3 found: ratio at 20 / 32 substeps is 1.427 / 1.167 with none, 1.434 / 1.172 at 10 N·s/m, 1.498 / 1.212 at 100, 2.13 / 1.61 at 1000. Beam damping alone also leaves the first bending mode (about 10 rad/s) ringing for over 40 s, because damping on stiff beams barely touches slow modes.
- **[decision]** Settling method: beam damping zero and a test-side drag `−10 · v` newtons per node through `add_force`. It is zero at rest, so it cannot move the settled shape (results identical at drag 5, 10 and 20). It lags one 60 Hz step and pumps the fastest modes above about 100 substeps, so the 128-substep test switches it off after 3 s. The solver's damping is unchanged; whether damping moves to a separate velocity pass stays open, not needed for this test.
- **[decision]** Tolerances, from the measurements: formula within 2% of exact (measured 1.5%); solver at 128 substeps within 2% of the formula (measured −0.5%: the formula's +1.5% and the solver's +1.0% partly cancel); at the rule's count, the law itself: excess over exact between `0.38 (ω h)²` and `0.46 (ω h)²` at 16, 20, 32 and 64 substeps (measured 0.415 to 0.43), and shrinking. Not the 10% guess: at the rule's count the error is about 43%, and a percentage would hide that it is a known law of the method.
- **[decision]** `c` stays 1.0. It is not a stability limit; it is the cost and accuracy dial, with excess about `0.4 c²` at the rule's count. Halving it (10%) doubles the solver cost and pushes the test truss to 40 substeps, past the maximum of 32. Raising the maximum and the game-wide stiffness scale are owner calls, so this is surfaced rather than changed.
- **[question]** Accept about 40% softer-than-nominal structures at `c = 1` (tune stiffness by feel through the stiffness scale), or buy accuracy with `c = 0.5` and a maximum of 64 substeps at twice the cost? Recommended: keep `c = 1` until the 1.7 benchmark gives the cost, then decide.

- **[action]** `core/tests/failure.rs` rewritten, 11 tests, expected values from the derived law: held 1.5 `F_y` gives acceleration `(F - F_y) / m` and `last_lambda = F_y` at 8, 16 and 32 substeps; load then unload leaves a permanent set with the same stiffness; parallel bars with different yields collapse at the sum of their yield forces; impact energy equals plastic work `F_y * set` in tension and in compression (error shrinks from 16 to 64 substeps); break happens once and the beam then carries nothing; break in compression; event order; broken beams skipped by the substep rule; determinism with hash coverage. ## 2026-11-16 (phase 1.5 damping fix)

- **[action]** Review of the force-cap commit found that the cap limited spring and damper together but moved `rest` as if the spring alone were at yield: a critically damped beam knocked at 0.5 m/s yielded at 0.33% strain, and a sudden 0.99 `F_y` load stretched it to 6.8%. The ROADMAP yield check and the layout doc's yield paragraph were therefore wrong. Fixed: damping is now a separate implicit velocity pass, `damp_beam`, run per beam in ascending order after each substep's velocity is derived. It moves the relative speed along the beam to `v / (1 + damping · h · w)`. `solve_distance` is now `Δλ = -C / (w + α)` with the cap on the spring alone; it no longer takes `damping` or the previous positions. `last_lambda` is the spring force only.
- **[action]** `core/tests/failure.rs`, 15 tests. New: damping does not count toward yield (a 0.5 m/s knock, and a 0.99 `F_y` step load at the rule's count and at 64 substeps, give no event and unchanged `rest` bits); tension then compression leaves the difference of the two sets; a beam between two pinned nodes breaks but never yields. Changed: the damped impact test and the break test check first-order convergence against the damped continuous reference; the parallel-bars collapse checks the flow speed `(F - F1 - F2) / (D1 + D2)` and springs at exactly `F1` and `F2`. The elastic-scene golden hash was re-recorded once, on purpose, since that scene is damped. `cargo test` passes, including the slow cantilever test in release; `pnpm test` and `pnpm typecheck` pass. Roadmap percentages unchanged.
- **[decision]** Damping as a velocity pass, chosen against the project goals (performant, semi-realistic, deterministic) from three measured options, on a sudden 0.99 `F_y` load and on two parallel bars at rest. (B) Damping kept inside the constraint, with only the spring part capped: no false yield, but the at-rest damper term made the weak bar carry 93% over its `F_y` at the rule's count (8.5% at 64 substeps). (H) A rate-limited cap: yielded with a 4.6% set at the rule's count. (V) The velocity pass: no false yield, and the weak bar is exact. V also costs one extra loop over the beams per substep with no square root, and it is exact for a still structure. It supersedes the 1.3 `γ` decision and answers the 1.4 open question about a separate damping pass.
- **[finding]** A damped beam held above yield now flows at a terminal speed, `(F - F_y) / damping` on a lone mass, rather than accelerating. The damper is not capped. An undamped beam still flows at `(F - F_y) / m`.
- **[finding]** The damping pass is first order in `damping · w · h`. On a critically damped test beam (120 N·s/m, 1 kg), the impact set and the one-step stretch read 4.9% and 3.4% high at 64 substeps, and the error halves each time the substeps double (19.4, 9.8, 4.9, 2.5, 1.3% from 16 to 256). Two dampers acting one after the other on one node add `D1 · D2 · h` to their sum (1.8% in the parallel-bars test).
- **[finding]** A beam between two pinned nodes is never solved, so it never yields; it can still break on strain if the pins move apart. Now documented in the layout doc.
- **[decision]** Layout doc edits, as binding as before: the `solve_distance` signature drops `damping`; Solver outline adds the damping pass before the clamps; the damping formula and the yield paragraph are rewritten.
- **[demo]** Snap demo regenerated (`-- snap`): snap-a peaks at 3.0% and springs back, no events; snap-b yields on step 119 and stays 0.21% longer (was 1.95%, inflated by the bug); snap-c yields on step 81 and snaps on step 105 (event value 0.157; was 91, because a damped beam now flows at a set speed rather than accelerating). Replaces the earlier [demo] numbers; no browser recheck this time.

## 2026-11-16 (phase 1.6)

Islands are now connected components of movable nodes over live beams, rebuilt every step, ordered by lowest movable node, with the substep count chosen per island. - **[decision]** Numerics, against performant, semi-realistic, deterministic. Fdlibm kernels over a lookup table or a short series: about 1 ulp, no tables to keep identical, and cheap. Range reduction is exact only while `|k| < 2^20`, so the domain is `|x| ≤ 1e6` rad; outside it, and for NaN or infinity, the result is NaN so the bad-number freeze catches a runaway angle instead of hiding it. `sin(-0.0)` returns `-0.0` so oddness holds bit for bit. A joint angle is nowhere near 1e6 and stored angles are to be wrapped.
- **[decision]** Benchmark scene, all values fixed in `bench.rs` and marked [assumption] where they stand in for a budget: 8 columns of 30 rings of 12 nodes, 11,136 beams and 2,880 nodes; radius 2 m, ring spacing 0.5 m, 2 kg nodes, 8 kg on the top ring, beam `EA` 500 kN, damping 50 N·s/m, yield 1%, break 5%; base ring pinned; a 10 N sideways gust per top node, period two seconds. The size is a stand-in for a busy scene until the owner sets the performance budget. Columns stand apart so each is its own island (8 islands, so the per-island substep rule and the island rebuild both get exercised). The scene is elastic only: it never yields, breaks or freezes, so the timing does not depend on when something tears (checked over 720 steps at both rules by an ignored test).
- **[finding]** A first version of the scene tore itself apart (9 and 16 islands, yield and break events from about step 333). Raising damping did not help, which pointed to a mechanism rather than a resonance: a tube with one diagonal per quad is a twist-and-crush mechanism (the Kresling pattern) and buckles under the top payload. Both diagonals in every quad fix it (worst strain under 0.35%); the spec's tank has cross-bracing too. Worth remembering for the part generators in milestone 4: a single diagonal is not a stiff tube.
